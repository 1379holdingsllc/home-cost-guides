How Much Does Airbnb Turnover Cleaning Cost? 2026 Price Guide

Updated 2026-10-19 · Figures are typical estimates reported by the sources cited at the end of this page.

If you host on Airbnb or Vrbo, cleaning is the cost you pay over and over. Every checkout means stripping beds, washing towels, scrubbing bathrooms, restocking toilet paper and coffee, and getting the place photo-ready before the next guest walks in, sometimes within a few hours. A turnover clean is not the same job as a regular house cleaning, and it isn't priced the same way. This guide pulls together what published cost guides report for 2026, explains which parts of a turnover drive the price, and shows how hosts use those numbers to set a cleaning fee that actually covers the work.

Quick answer

HomeGuide reports that most Airbnb hosts pay $75–$250 per turnover cleaning, with a national average of about $150, and large homes with four or more bedrooms running $250–$400 or more. Hourly turnover cleaners charge about $25–$65 per hour. Angi lists vacation and property rental cleaning as an add-on service from house cleaning companies at $175–$500, and Angi's Atlanta house cleaning guide puts vacation or rental property cleaning in Atlanta at $190–$540. These are typical estimates from those sources, not quotes. The size of your unit, how much laundry the cleaner handles and how tight the checkout-to-check-in window is will move your price within these ranges.

Why turnovers cost more than regular cleaning

HomeGuide says short-term rental cleaning typically runs 20%–40% more than a comparable residential cleaning. The extra money pays for hotel-style work that a weekly house cleaner doesn't do: restocking supplies, laundering and changing every set of linens, staging the rooms the same way every time, and following a detailed checklist. The cleaner is also the last person in the unit before a paying guest arrives, so they're often expected to spot damage, missing items or maintenance problems and report them with photos. That inspection role is a big part of what you're buying.

Cost by bedroom count

Bedrooms are the single biggest price driver because each one adds a bed to strip and remake and, usually, more laundry. HomeGuide's 2026 figures:

HomeGuide also breaks costs out by square footage, from $50–$85 for a unit under 500 square feet to $325–$450 or more for homes over 3,500 square feet. Each additional bathroom typically adds $20–$40, and extra sleeping areas such as sofa beds or bunk rooms add $15–$30 each because of the extra linens. A two-bedroom that sleeps eight with bunks and a pullout is a bigger job than a two-bedroom with two queen beds, so describe the beds, not just the bedrooms, when you ask for a quote.

HomeGuide says flat-rate pricing is the most popular with hosts because it makes the cleaning fee easy to set and the expense predictable. The trade-off is that a cleaner may ask for an extra charge after a guest leaves the place in bad shape. Hourly pricing can save money when guests are tidy, but HomeGuide notes a standard two-bedroom turnover takes about 1.5 to 3 hours and a messy checkout can stretch it to 4 or 5. Per-room pricing suits hosts who sometimes lock off part of the home.

The add-ons that change the bill

Most of the difference between a cheap turnover quote and an expensive one is in the extras. HomeGuide lists these typical adders:

  • Same-day turnovers: $20–$50 rush fee when one guest checks out and another checks in within hours.
  • Laundry done on site: $15–$40, plus significant time waiting on the washer and dryer.
  • Professional linen service: $30–$75 per turnover, which delivers fresh sets and can cut 30 to 60 minutes off the clean.
  • Hot tub: $25–$50 per turnover. Outdoor areas with furniture to wipe down and stage add $15–$30.
  • Pet-friendly listings: $15–$40 for extra vacuuming, lint rolling and odor checks.
  • Cleaner-supplied products: $10–$25 more than if you supply them.
  • Weekend and holiday turnovers: 10%–25% more.
  • Deep cleaning needs: $50–$200 or more added to a turnover.

HomeGuide also says urban and resort markets cost 20%–50% more than smaller cities. Run through this list before you collect quotes and decide which items each cleaner should include, or you'll be comparing a bare clean against a full-service turnover.

What a turnover should include

HomeGuide describes a typical turnover checklist room by room. In the kitchen, that means washing dishes, wiping counters, cleaning appliances inside and out and restocking supplies. Bathrooms get the toilet, shower, tub and sink scrubbed, towels replaced and toiletries restocked. Bedrooms get beds stripped and remade with fresh linens, surfaces dusted and trash emptied. Living areas are vacuumed, mopped and dusted, with pillows and decor arranged and remotes and switches wiped. General tasks include taking out trash, locking windows, setting the thermostat and reporting damage. Restocking covers toilet paper, paper towels, soap, shampoo and coffee, and staging covers welcome items, folded towels and the house guidebook. HomeGuide notes many experienced cleaners photograph the unit after each clean, which protects both host and cleaner if a guest later disputes the condition.

The best way to make quotes comparable is to write your own checklist with photos of how each room should look when it's done, and send the same list to every cleaner.

Budget for deep cleans too

Turnovers keep a unit guest-ready, but they don't reach everything. HomeGuide recommends a deep clean every 3 to 6 months, covering behind and under furniture, windows inside and out, carpet shampooing, air vents and grout, at $200–$500 or more depending on size. Spread across your bookings, that's a real per-stay cost. Leave it out of your math and the cleaning fee will look profitable when it isn't.

Setting your guest cleaning fee

HomeGuide puts the average U.S. Airbnb cleaning fee charged to guests at about $75–$150, and describes three common approaches: pass the full cost through to the guest, charge a moderate fee and build the rest into the nightly rate, or charge no cleaning fee and price like a hotel. HomeGuide notes hosts who charge cleaning fees above $200 report more complaints about cleanliness, because guests who pay a big fee inspect harder. A flat fee also matters more on short stays. HomeGuide's example: a $150 fee adds $150 a night to a one-night stay but only about $21 a night to a seven-night stay. That's why many hosts pair a reasonable fee with a two- or three-night minimum.

DIY or hire a cleaner?

HomeGuide compares four options. Cleaning it yourself costs about $10–$30 in supplies per turnover and gives you full quality control, but it's hard to keep up with back-to-back bookings. Independent cleaners run about $60–$200 and tend to be flexible, but backup coverage can be a problem. Cleaning companies charge about $100–$300 or more and usually have backup staff. Services that specialize in short-term rentals cost about $100–$350 or more and bring turnover checklists and photo documentation. DIY makes the most sense when you live close by, host occasionally and have a small unit.

Ways to lower turnover costs

  • Set a minimum stay. HomeGuide estimates a two- to three-night minimum can mean 30%–50% fewer turnovers.
  • Ask for a volume rate. HomeGuide says many cleaners discount 10%–20% for hosts who provide steady work and pay promptly.
  • Supply your own products to save about $10–$25 per clean.
  • Simplify the decor. Fewer throw pillows and knickknacks mean less time and less breakage.
  • Ask guests to help. HomeGuide says asking guests to start a load of laundry or take out the trash can save 15 to 30 minutes.
  • Line up a backup cleaner who knows the unit, so a sick day doesn't turn into a last-minute premium booking.

Tipping

HomeGuide says tipping isn't required, but many hosts tip $10–$25 per clean or give holiday bonuses, especially after a rough checkout or during busy seasons. It adds that fair base pay matters more than tips for keeping a good turnover cleaner.

How to compare Airbnb turnover cleaning quotes

Turnover quotes are only comparable when every cleaner prices the same job. Send each one the same property details and checklist, then ask:

  • Flat rate or hourly? If hourly, how many hours do they expect for your unit, and is there a minimum?
  • Beds and sleeping spots. Quote by the number of beds, sofa beds and bunks, not just bedrooms. HomeGuide says each extra sleeping area adds $15–$30.
  • Laundry. Washed on site, taken off site, or swapped through a linen service? Who supplies the spare sets?
  • Restocking. Do they restock from your supply closet, and will they tell you when supplies run low? Do they bring cleaning products or use yours?
  • Same-day and weekend rates. What's the rush fee for a tight checkout-to-check-in window, and do weekend or holiday turns cost more?
  • Photos and damage reports. Do they photograph the unit after each clean and report damage, missing items and maintenance problems right away?
  • Bad checkouts. On a flat rate, what happens when a guest leaves a big mess? Get the extra charge in writing.
  • Backup coverage. Who cleans when they're sick or booked? HomeGuide recommends asking for references from other hosts.

A very low quote usually leaves out laundry, restocking or same-day turns. That can be fine if you handle those yourself, but compare it against quotes that cover the same work.

Notes for metro Atlanta homeowners

Angi's Atlanta house cleaning guide says add-on services such as rental property turnovers are popular in the city because of the short-term rental market, and lists vacation or rental property cleaning in Atlanta at $190–$540, with Atlanta add-on prices running slightly above national averages. Angi puts Atlanta's average hourly cleaning rate at about $54, ranging from $28 to $85. Hosts inside the city limits also need a short-term rental license. According to the City of Atlanta short-term rental page, the license costs $150, is valid for 12 months, must be renewed every year and can take up to ten business days to issue. A short-term rental is defined as a residential unit rented for no more than 30 consecutive days. The required STR agent must display emergency contact numbers in an easily visible place in the unit and list the license number on every listing, so make sure your turnover checklist keeps that posted information in place after each clean. Cities and counties outside Atlanta set their own rules, so check locally. Spring pine pollen is a cleaning issue too. It settles on porches, patio furniture, window sills and floors near doors, and guests notice. Expect turnovers to take a little longer during heavy pollen weeks if outdoor furniture and entryways are on the checklist, and watch the Atlanta Allergy & Asthma pollen count when planning a deep clean.

Frequently asked questions

How long does an Airbnb turnover take?

HomeGuide says a standard two-bedroom turnover takes about 1.5 to 3 hours, and a messy checkout can stretch that to 4 or 5 hours. On-site laundry adds time because the cleaner waits on the washer and dryer.

Should my cleaning fee match what I pay the cleaner?

Many hosts set it at or slightly above the cleaner's charge, but HomeGuide describes partial absorption and no-fee pricing as common alternatives. Whichever you choose, include laundry, supplies and periodic deep cleans in your real cost.

How often does a short-term rental need a deep clean?

HomeGuide recommends a deep clean every 3 to 6 months on top of regular turnovers, at about $200 to $500 or more depending on the size of the property.
