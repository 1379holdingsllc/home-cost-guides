Office Cleaning Cost Per Square Foot (2026 Guide for Small Businesses)

Updated 2026-10-16 · Figures are typical estimates reported by the sources cited at the end of this page.

For small business owners, office cleaning is a recurring line item, so a small difference per visit adds up over a year. This guide explains how janitorial companies price, what benchmark figures exist, and how to write a scope so bids are comparable.

Quick answer

Benchmark data published by CleanQuote suggests standard office cleaning commonly runs on the order of $0.07–$0.15 per square foot per visit, which at five visits a week works out to roughly $0.15–$0.32 per square foot per month. Angi also publishes a commercial cleaning cost guide. These are rough benchmarks; restroom count, flooring, traffic and local wages change pricing significantly, so treat them as a starting point, not a quote.

How janitorial companies price

Per square foot

Most common for offices. The contractor estimates how many square feet a worker can clean per hour for your type of space, then builds labor hours, supplies, overhead and margin into a monthly price.

Per hour

Common for small offices, one-time deep cleans, and post-event cleanups.

Flat monthly rate

Often how the price is presented to you, even if it was calculated per square foot behind the scenes.

What drives the price

  • Frequency. Five nights a week costs more per month but less per visit than once a week, because each visit is lighter.
  • Restrooms and kitchens. These take far more time per square foot than open office areas.
  • Flooring. Carpet vacuuming is fast; hard floors needing mopping, and periodic strip-and-wax or carpet extraction, add cost.
  • Headcount and traffic. More people means more trash, more restroom use, more touchpoints.
  • Specialty requirements. Medical offices, labs and childcare spaces may need specific disinfection protocols.
  • Supplies. Confirm whether paper towels, toilet paper, soap and liners are included or billed separately.

Writing a scope of work

The best way to compare bids is to give every company the same written scope. A simple version:

TaskFrequency
Empty trash and recycling, replace linersEach visit
Clean and disinfect restrooms, restock suppliesEach visit
Vacuum carpeted traffic areasEach visit
Mop hard floorsEach visit
Wipe break room counters, sink, microwave exteriorEach visit
Dust reachable horizontal surfacesWeekly
Clean interior entry glassWeekly
Full vacuum including edgesWeekly
High dusting (vents, tops of partitions)Monthly
Carpet extraction / floor refinishingQuarterly or as quoted

Contract terms to read carefully

  • Length of term and auto-renewal language.
  • Notice period to cancel.
  • How missed visits or poor quality are handled.
  • Price escalation clauses.
  • Key and alarm code procedures.
  • Proof of general liability insurance and, ideally, a janitorial bond.
  • Whether work is subcontracted, and who is responsible.

Day porter vs. night cleaning

Most offices are cleaned after hours. Higher-traffic sites sometimes add a daytime porter to restock restrooms and keep lobbies tidy. That's billed hourly on top of the night service.

Red flags in a janitorial bid

A bid far below others can mean the contractor will under-staff the job or skip tasks. Ask how many labor hours per week they are budgeting. If one company plans 10 hours and another plans 20 for the same scope, the cheap one may not be realistic.

Small office example (illustrative only)

Using the benchmark range above, a 3,000-sq-ft office cleaned three times a week at $0.07–$0.15 per sq ft per visit would be roughly $210–$450 per visit before minimums. That's just arithmetic on the benchmark — actual bids will reflect your restroom count, flooring and local wages, and many companies have a monthly minimum.

Questions to ask

  1. How many labor hours per week are in your price?
  2. Are supplies included?
  3. Who supervises quality, and how often do you inspect?
  4. How do you screen and train staff?
  5. Can you provide a certificate of insurance naming my business?
  6. What's the cancellation policy?

How to compare office cleaning quotes

Commercial cleaning bids are usually monthly contracts, so small differences in scope add up over a year. Compare:

  • A written scope by frequency. A good bid lists daily, weekly and monthly tasks separately (for example, trash daily, vacuum weekly, high dusting monthly). Without that, two bids can't be compared.
  • Restrooms and break rooms. These drive most of the labor. Make sure every bidder walked them and priced the same number of visits.
  • Supplies and consumables. Ask whether paper goods, soap and liners are included or billed separately. Then compare totals.
  • Staffing and keys. Who will be in your building after hours, how are they screened, and how are keys or access codes handled?
  • Insurance and bonding. Ask for certificates naming your business. Bonding matters when crews work unsupervised.
  • Contract terms. Look for the notice period to cancel, price-increase clauses and how missed tasks are reported and fixed.

Ask for a short trial period or a 30-day cancellation clause. A cleaner who's confident in their work rarely objects.

Notes for metro Atlanta homeowners

Metro Atlanta has everything from Midtown high-rises to suburban office parks and small storefront offices, and access rules vary a lot. Multi-tenant buildings may require cleaners to carry specific insurance, sign in with security or use the building's freight elevator at set times, which can limit when crews can work. Spring pollen and red-clay dirt tracked in from parking lots add to entry-mat and hard-floor work. A good bid should mention walk-off mats and entry floor care. If your office is in a building with a property manager, ask whether the landlord's janitorial contract already covers common areas so you don't pay for them twice.

Frequently asked questions

How often should an office be cleaned?

Many small offices use two to five visits per week depending on headcount and restrooms. Low-traffic offices may need less.

Should supplies be included?

Either is fine; just make sure every bid treats them the same so you can compare totals.

Are these prices guaranteed?

No. All figures on this page are typical ranges reported by the sources listed below, presented as estimates. Your actual price depends on your property, your location and the provider you choose.
