Dryer Vent Cleaning Cost (2026): Prices by Vent Location

Updated 2026-10-13 · Figures are typical estimates reported by the sources cited at the end of this page.

Dryer vent cleaning is a small job with an outsized safety payoff. Lint is highly flammable, and a clogged vent forces the dryer to run hotter and longer. Angi cites FEMA guidance that lint and debris buildup in vents is a fire hazard. The good news: this is one of the least expensive home maintenance services.

Quick answer

Angi reports an average of about $145, with jobs ranging roughly $75–$340. HomeGuide lists a national average near $130 and $80–$185 for standard vents, with roof vents at $150–$250. The biggest single factor is where the vent exits the house.

Warning signs your vent needs cleaning

  • Clothes take more than one normal cycle to dry.
  • The dryer or laundry room gets unusually hot.
  • A burning smell when the dryer runs.
  • Lint collecting around the outside vent flap, or the flap doesn't open when the dryer is running.
  • Visible lint behind the dryer or a crushed transition hose.
  • Your dryer shows a "check vent" or airflow warning.

Why roof and long vents cost more

A short, straight run out a first-floor wall can be cleaned in well under an hour. Vents that go up through the attic and out the roof require roof access, fall protection and often removing a roof cap. Long runs with several 90-degree turns trap more lint and need more brush work. Townhomes and condos often have long vertical runs for exactly this reason.

Birds, rodents and other surprises

Exterior vent openings without a proper guard can attract nesting birds and rodents. Removing a nest adds cost, and if pests are inside walls you may need a pest control company too. After cleaning, ask about a vent guard designed for dryers — it should keep animals out without trapping lint (fine screens can clog and are generally discouraged by installers).

Materials matter

The flexible hose between the dryer and the wall is called the transition duct. White vinyl or thin foil accordion hose crushes easily and traps lint. Many installers and appliance manuals call for rigid or semi-rigid metal duct. If your technician recommends replacing the transition duct, that's usually an inexpensive upgrade worth considering. Always follow your dryer manufacturer's installation instructions for maximum duct length and materials.

DIY or hire out?

For a short first-floor run, a DIY rotary brush kit can work: disconnect the dryer, brush from both ends, vacuum the debris, reconnect and test airflow. Hire a pro if the vent goes through the roof, the run is long or hidden in walls, you suspect a nest, or the duct is damaged. Pros typically use rotary brushes plus high-powered vacuums or air whips and can measure airflow before and after.

How often to clean

Once a year is the common recommendation for an average household. Large families, homes with pets, and long vent runs may need it more often. Clean the lint trap after every load regardless; it only catches part of the lint.

Questions to ask

  1. Is roof access included in the price?
  2. Do you clean the full run, including the transition hose and the dryer's internal lint chute?
  3. Will you check airflow before and after?
  4. What does nest or pest debris removal cost if you find it?
  5. Are you insured for roof work?

Bundling to save money

Some companies offer dryer vent cleaning as an add-on to air duct cleaning or a chimney service. If you're already booking one of those, ask for the add-on price. Landlords and HOAs with several units can often negotiate a per-unit rate.

Bottom line

At published averages in the low-to-mid hundreds, an annual dryer vent cleaning is one of the cheapest safety items on a home maintenance list. Expect to pay at the lower end for a ground-level wall vent and at the upper end for a roof termination or a vent with a nest in it.

How to compare dryer vent cleaning quotes

National averages are a starting point, not a price. The most reliable way to know what dryer vent cleaning costs for your property is to get two or three written quotes and compare them line by line. When you ask for a quote, give every company the same information: the size of the job, photos, access details (stairs, gates, height, parking), and what you want included. Then check each quote for the same things:

  • Scope: exactly which areas, items or tasks are included, and which are excluded.
  • Pricing basis: flat rate, per unit (square foot, room, foot, load), or hourly — and any minimum charge.
  • Extras: surcharges for heavy soil, height, disposal, travel, or after-hours work.
  • Insurance: general liability coverage, and proof you can verify.
  • Guarantee: what happens if you're not satisfied.
  • Payment terms: deposit required (if any), accepted payment methods, and when the balance is due.

The lowest number isn't automatically the best value. A slightly higher quote that includes disposal, insurance and a re-service guarantee can cost less in the end than a low quote with surprise add-ons. Likewise, be cautious of any company that won't put its price in writing, demands full payment in cash up front, or can't give you a business name and phone number.

Frequently asked questions

How long does dryer vent cleaning take?

A short, accessible vent can often be done in under an hour; roof and long vents take longer.

Is the lint trap enough?

No. The lint screen catches much of the lint, but some passes through into the duct over time.

Are these prices guaranteed?

No. All figures on this page are typical ranges reported by the sources listed below, presented as estimates. Your actual price depends on your property, your location and the provider you choose.
