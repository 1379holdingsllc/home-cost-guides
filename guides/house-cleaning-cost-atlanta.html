How Much Does House Cleaning Cost in Atlanta? 2026 Price Guide

Updated 2026-10-17 · Figures are typical estimates reported by the sources cited at the end of this page.

Hiring a house cleaner in Atlanta looks simple until the quotes come back. One company quotes a flat $150, another wants $54 an hour per cleaner, and a third won't give a number until it walks through the house. They may all be fair. Cleaners price by the hour, by the visit, by square footage or by bedroom and bathroom count, and the type of cleaning (standard, deep or move-out) changes the price more than anything else. This guide pulls together Atlanta-specific figures and national figures from published cost guides so you can see what a reasonable price looks like for your home.

Quick answer

Angi's Atlanta guide reports an average of about $187 per visit for professional house cleaning in Atlanta, with most homeowners paying $130–$252. Angi puts the Atlanta hourly average at about $54, with a range of $28–$85. For comparison, Angi's national guide reports a 2026 national average of $176, with most people paying $118–$238, and HomeGuide lists a national average of about $200 with a typical range of $120–$280. Angi says Atlanta's higher cost of living pushes local labor rates slightly above other cities. All figures are typical estimates from those sources, not quotes.

Standard, deep or move-out

This is the biggest swing in any quote. HomeGuide describes a standard clean as mopping, dusting, vacuuming, emptying trash, and cleaning sinks, faucets, mirrors, appliance exteriors and toilets. A deep clean adds the parts that get skipped week to week: baseboards, under furniture, inside appliances, and tile and grout. Move-out cleaning is a deep clean of an empty home, usually including the inside of cabinets and appliances. Angi's Atlanta guide lists average totals of about $293 for a standard clean, $488 for a deep clean and $684 for a move-out clean, and says a four-hour deep clean at the average hourly rate would cost about $215. Those averages reflect a wide mix of home sizes, so use the size and room tables above for your own estimate.

Expect the first visit with a new cleaner to cost more. HomeGuide says that with a weekly service, the first cleaning takes longer and costs more, because the cleaner is catching up on everything that built up before.

Hourly rates and crew size

Angi says Atlanta cleaners charge about $28–$54 per cleaner per hour depending on the job and whether you hire through an agency or an individual, and that companies often send two people, which works out to roughly $108 an hour on average for the visit. HomeGuide's national comparison puts cleaning companies at $75–$100 per hour for two workers, often with a two-hour minimum, and independent cleaners at $20–$50 per hour per worker. A two-person crew for two hours and a single cleaner for four hours can cost about the same and cover about the same work, so compare totals, not hourly rates.

Company or independent cleaner?

HomeGuide puts a typical company visit at $150–$250 and an independent cleaner at $135–$180. In HomeGuide's comparison, companies are more likely to be licensed, insured and bonded, use signed contracts and keep set prices, while independents tend to be more flexible on scheduling and price. Whichever you choose, HomeGuide recommends checking reviews, asking about a re-clean guarantee, doing a walkthrough so the cleaner sees the whole house before quoting, and putting valuables away.

Tipping

Angi's Atlanta guide suggests asking the company whether tips are expected and whether they go to the cleaners, and says 10–20 percent is a kind tip for good work, especially after a deep clean or around the holidays. HomeGuide gives a similar 10–15 percent rule of thumb.

How to compare Atlanta house cleaning quotes

Cleaning quotes come in different shapes (hourly, flat rate, per room), so turn every one into a total for the same job. Give each company the same list and ask:

  • Type of clean. Standard, deep or move-out, and exactly what's on their checklist for each. Baseboards, inside the fridge and oven, and interior windows are where lists differ most.
  • First-visit price vs. recurring price. HomeGuide says first visits take longer. Get both numbers.
  • Crew size and time. How many cleaners and how many hours. A per-hour rate means little without both.
  • Supplies. Do they bring products and equipment? HomeGuide says supplying your own can save $5–$10 a visit at some companies.
  • Pets and allergies. Shedding pets add time. Mention them up front, and ask about pet-safe or fragrance-free products if needed.
  • Re-clean policy. If something is missed, will they come back free, and how soon?
  • Cancellation and lockout fees. What happens if you reschedule late or nobody can get in?
  • Same cleaner each time? Consistency usually means better results on recurring service.

Compare the totals for the same checklist. The lowest hourly rate isn't always the cheapest visit.

Notes for metro Atlanta homeowners

Angi's Atlanta guide notes that add-on services such as rental property turnovers, wall washing and post-construction cleanup are popular in the city, partly because of the short-term rental market, and that Atlanta prices for larger add-on jobs tend to run a little above national averages. If you rent out a unit, price turnover cleaning separately from your own home's regular service. Atlanta's long pollen season and humid summers both affect cleaning. Yellow pine pollen coats window sills, porches and floors near doors in spring, so a deep clean is easier to time after the heavy pollen has passed. Angi also mentions lingering mildew smells from the city's humidity as something a deep clean addresses. Bathrooms and closets in older in-town homes with weak ventilation are the usual spots. Red clay tracked in from yards and new construction stains grout and light carpet, so mention it if it's a problem. Finally, traffic and distance matter for scheduling. Cleaners covering a wide area may set minimums or travel fees for addresses far from their base, so ask about both when you book in the suburbs or across town.

Frequently asked questions

Do house cleaners in Atlanta bring their own supplies?

Many do, but not all. Angi says some services ask the homeowner to provide products and basic equipment, while others bring everything. Ask when you book so the quotes you're comparing include the same thing.

How often should I book a cleaner?

It depends on your budget and household. Weekly service has the lowest per-visit price, and Angi suggests booking monthly or every other month if you want to cut costs while still getting regular help.

Is a deep clean necessary before recurring service?

Often, yes. Many companies start new clients with a deep clean or a longer first visit, and HomeGuide says the first cleaning on a weekly plan takes longer and costs more.

Are these prices guaranteed?

No. All figures on this page are typical ranges reported by the sources listed below, presented as estimates. Your actual price depends on your property, your location and the provider you choose.
