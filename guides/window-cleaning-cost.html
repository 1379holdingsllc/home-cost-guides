Window Cleaning Cost (2026): Per Pane, Per Window and Per Home

Updated 2026-10-13 · Figures are typical estimates reported by the sources cited at the end of this page.

Window cleaning sounds simple to price until you realize companies count differently: per pane, per window, per side, or per hour. This guide explains the published averages and the counting methods so you can compare quotes.

Quick answer

Angi's 2026 data puts the average professional window cleaning job at about $221, with most homeowners paying roughly $150–$302. Industry pricing guides commonly describe residential per-pane rates in the mid single digits for standard, easy-access glass. Treat per-pane figures as rough benchmarks and ask each company how they count.

Pane vs. window vs. side

A standard double-hung window has two sashes — upper and lower — so many companies call it two panes. Cleaning both sides (inside and outside) is two "sides" of each pane. A quote of "$X per window, inside and out" and "$Y per pane, outside only" are measuring very different amounts of work. Before comparing, convert each quote to the same unit for your actual windows.

What makes some windows cost more

  • Height. Second-story windows need ladders or water-fed poles; third-story or above may need lifts.
  • French and divided-lite windows. Many small panes take much longer than one large pane.
  • Hard water stains and paint/construction residue. Mineral deposits and post-construction cleanup require special treatment and razor work.
  • Screens and tracks. Washing screens and cleaning tracks/sills is often an add-on.
  • Skylights and sunrooms. Priced separately due to access.
  • Interior access. Furniture, blinds and window treatments slow down inside cleaning.

Outside only vs. inside and out

Many homeowners book exterior-only cleaning more often (pollen, rain spots) and interior cleaning once a year. Exterior-only is cheaper and doesn't require you to be home. Interior adds time and requires furniture access.

Water-fed poles vs. squeegee

Water-fed pole systems use purified water that dries spot-free, letting crews clean upper floors from the ground. Traditional squeegee work gives a crisp finish and handles stubborn spots. Either is legitimate; what matters is the result and safety. Ask which method the company uses for upper floors.

Storefront and commercial windows

Small businesses often book recurring storefront cleaning weekly, biweekly or monthly. These routes are usually priced per visit for ground-level glass and are often far cheaper per visit than residential whole-home jobs because the work is fast and recurring. High-rise and lift work is a specialty market with its own safety requirements.

How often to clean

Once or twice a year is common for homes. Homes near the coast, construction, busy roads or heavy pollen may want quarterly exterior cleaning. In the Southeast, many homeowners schedule a cleaning after spring pollen season.

DIY tips

  1. Clean on an overcast day; direct sun dries solution into streaks.
  2. Use a mild solution (a few drops of dish soap in a bucket of water) and a real squeegee rather than paper towels.
  3. Wipe the squeegee blade after each pass.
  4. Do not stand on the top of a ladder or lean out of upper windows. Hire out anything you can't reach safely from the ground or the first floor.

Questions to ask

  1. How do you count panes, and does your price include both sides?
  2. Are screens, sills and tracks included?
  3. How do you handle upper floors?
  4. Do you offer a recurring discount?
  5. What is your policy if streaks remain?
  6. Are you insured for ladder work and for damage to glass?

Ways to save

  • Book exterior-only between full cleanings.
  • Clean screens yourself in the driveway before the crew arrives.
  • Bundle with gutter cleaning or house washing.
  • Sign up for a recurring schedule.

Bottom line

Most homes land in the low-to-mid hundreds of dollars per full cleaning according to Angi's data. Count your windows, decide inside/outside, and ask companies to quote on the same basis.

How to compare window cleaning quotes

National averages are a starting point, not a price. The most reliable way to know what window cleaning costs for your property is to get two or three written quotes and compare them line by line. When you ask for a quote, give every company the same information: the size of the job, photos, access details (stairs, gates, height, parking), and what you want included. Then check each quote for the same things:

  • Scope: exactly which areas, items or tasks are included, and which are excluded.
  • Pricing basis: flat rate, per unit (square foot, room, foot, load), or hourly — and any minimum charge.
  • Extras: surcharges for heavy soil, height, disposal, travel, or after-hours work.
  • Insurance: general liability coverage, and proof you can verify.
  • Guarantee: what happens if you're not satisfied.
  • Payment terms: deposit required (if any), accepted payment methods, and when the balance is due.

The lowest number isn't automatically the best value. A slightly higher quote that includes disposal, insurance and a re-service guarantee can cost less in the end than a low quote with surprise add-ons. Likewise, be cautious of any company that won't put its price in writing, demands full payment in cash up front, or can't give you a business name and phone number.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to be home?

Not for exterior-only cleaning. For interior cleaning, someone needs to provide access.

Is rain bad after a window cleaning?

Rain itself is fairly clean, but it can redeposit dust and pollen. Many crews will still work in light rain on exterior glass.

Do window cleaners clean screens?

Many offer screen cleaning as an add-on, either a quick wipe or a full wash. Damaged screens can usually be rescreened for an extra fee. Ask whether screens are included in the per-window price before you compare quotes.

Are these prices guaranteed?

No. All figures on this page are typical ranges reported by the sources listed below, presented as estimates. Your actual price depends on your property, your location and the provider you choose.
