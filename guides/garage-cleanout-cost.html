How Much Does a Garage Cleanout Cost? 2026 Price Guide

Updated 2026-10-19 · Figures are typical estimates reported by the sources cited at the end of this page.

The garage is where things go when nobody wants to decide what to do with them. A few years of moving boxes, broken furniture, old paint cans, outgrown bikes and a treadmill nobody uses, and suddenly the car is parked in the driveway. Paying someone to clear it out can mean anything from a crew that loads up what you've already set aside to a full service that sorts every shelf, hauls the junk, sweeps the floor and puts what's left back in order. This guide covers what published cost guides report for 2026, why quotes vary so much, and which items in a garage tend to add fees.

Quick answer

Angi reports a 2026 national average of about $450 for professional garage clean-out services, with most homeowners spending $200–$700. Angi says you might pay about $100 to have a couple of old appliances collected, or up to $800 for a crew to sort and remove a big pile of junk from a two-car garage. HomeGuide puts garage cleaning services at $150–$1,000 on average, depending on garage size, the type of cleaning and services included, the amount and type of junk, and location. These are typical estimates from those sources, not quotes.

Size sets the ceiling on how much a garage can hold, and both sources price by it:

Cost by how much junk you have

Many haulers price by volume: by the number of boxes and bulky items, by cubic yards, or by how much of a truck the load fills. Angi's 2026 figures:

For scale, Angi says a pickup truck holds about 3.5 cubic yards of junk, which covers most garages short of a jam-packed double or triple. Angi also notes that when a garage is piled so high it's hard to move around, crews may charge a premium for the extra safety and access work.

The level of service is the other big reason two quotes for the same garage can be hundreds of dollars apart. HomeGuide breaks it into three tiers:

Angi describes the same split: some companies only lift clutter that's already been sorted, while others go through everything with you to decide what to keep, donate or throw out, at extra cost, and may finish with a final cleaning. HomeGuide notes full-service companies may sweep, mop or pressure wash the floor, dust shelves and clean walls. If a quote seems low, the first question is which of these tiers it covers.

Items that add fees

What's in the garage matters as much as how much. Standard household items such as boxes, small furniture and clothing are the easiest to remove. Angi puts standard non-bulky waste from a single garage at about $150–$300. Heavier and regulated items cost more:

  • Paint, chemicals and automotive fluids. HomeGuide says hazardous materials need special handling, with added disposal fees of about $10–$50 or more. Angi lists VOC paints and solvents among the items that push a quote higher.
  • Electronics. Computers, TVs and some appliances often have to go to certified e-waste facilities, and HomeGuide lists an extra $5–$40 per item.
  • Appliances and mattresses. Angi says removing a mattress averages about $100 and an appliance about the same, while appliances containing chemicals, such as refrigerators, can cost more.
  • Heavy items. HomeGuide notes large furniture, appliances and exercise equipment take extra labor.
  • Local disposal fees. HomeGuide says disposal fees vary by municipality, with some areas adding $50–$150.

Angi also says location can add 10%–20% on top of national averages, with busy urban markets and remote rural areas both tending to cost more.

Add-ons people bundle with a cleanout

Many garage cleanout companies also offer cleaning and organizing, and Angi says bundling can unlock a discount. Angi's typical prices for common add-ons:

  • Furniture removal: $75–$250
  • House cleaning: $120–$235
  • Professional organizer: $250–$835

If you're clearing the garage before a floor coating, a move or a sale, think about what comes next. Once everything is out, the floor is the easiest it will ever be to clean, seal or coat, and it's a natural time to pressure wash oil stains or schedule the work.

How long it takes

HomeGuide estimates 1 to 4 hours for a single-car garage, 4 to 6 hours for a two-car garage and 6 to 8 hours for a three-car garage, with heavily cluttered garages taking one to two full days. Angi gives a similar picture, from a couple of hours to a day or two. On an hourly quote, the time estimate is the price estimate, so ask for both.

DIY vs. hiring a pro

You can clear a garage yourself, and the cheapest version of this job is the one where you do the sorting. Angi's top cost-saving tip is to go through everything and pile it up ready for collection, so you only need a junk removal service rather than a full clean-out company. The case for hiring is mostly about time and heavy lifting. Angi points to faster cleaning and organizing, possibly lower rates on dumpster rental and junk disposal, avoiding injuries from lifting heavy items, and access to add-ons like furniture removal and cleaning. HomeGuide lists the situations where pros are most useful: getting a house ready to sell, estate cleanouts, hoarding situations, clearing space for garage renovations and seasonal organizing.

Ways to keep the cost down

  • Sort first. Make keep, donate, sell and toss piles before anyone arrives.
  • Clear a path. Angi says simply making sure the crew has safe access can sometimes reduce costs.
  • Sell and donate. A garage sale or online listings for big-ticket items can offset the bill. Angi notes charities may pick up working furniture or appliances.
  • Separate the hazardous stuff. Paint and chemicals often cost extra with a hauler. Dropping them at a recycling center yourself can be cheaper (see the Atlanta notes below).
  • Book ahead. Angi suggests scheduling further in advance to reduce costs where possible.
  • Get several quotes. HomeGuide recommends at least three, and confirming the quote includes pickup, loading, taxes and disposal fees.

How to compare garage cleanout quotes

Garage cleanout bids vary mostly because companies quote different scopes. Before comparing totals, get each company to answer:

  • Which service tier? Haul-only, sorting plus removal, or sorting, organizing and floor cleaning. HomeGuide prices these as separate tiers.
  • How is volume measured? By item, by cubic yard or by fraction of a truck? Ask for the estimate in the same unit from every company.
  • Disposal fees. HomeGuide recommends confirming the quote includes pickup, loading, taxes and disposal, including hazardous waste and e-waste fees.
  • Exclusions. Which items won't they take, such as paint, chemicals, propane tanks or tires, and what do the ones they will take cost?
  • Price changes on the day. HomeGuide suggests asking whether the price could rise on service day and what the maximum would be.
  • Where it goes. Do they donate and recycle, or send everything to the landfill? Ask for donation receipts if you want them.
  • Condition at the end. Is sweeping included, or does the job end when the last box is on the truck?

Photos of the whole garage, including shelves and the attic or loft space, help companies quote accurately without a visit.

Notes for metro Atlanta homeowners

Paint and household chemicals are where Atlanta homeowners can save the most on a garage cleanout. Live Thrive's CHaRM (Center for Hard to Recycle Materials) at 1110 Hill St SE in Atlanta, with a second location in Decatur, accepts paint, stains, paint thinner, pesticides, pool chemicals, motor oil, gasoline and similar household hazardous materials in their original containers. According to the Live Thrive CHaRM accepted-items list, paint and chemicals are weighed together, the first 20 pounds are free for residents, and the rest is charged by the pound (70 cents a pound for loads up to 100 pounds). The same list shows fees for other common garage items, including $3 per car tire without a rim, $15–$20 per large appliance and $25 per TV or monitor, while most other electronics are taken free. CHaRM does not accept propane tanks. Live Thrive asks visitors to make an appointment. For furniture and appliances, City of Atlanta single-family residents get 12 free scheduled bulk collections a year through the ATL311 Bulk Collection Request process, each limited to 8 cubic yards and placed at the curb in front of the property no earlier than 7 p.m. the night before. ATL311 says the city won't collect auto parts, tires or building materials, and charges a fee once the 12 free pickups are used. Combining a city bulk pickup for big items, a CHaRM drop-off for chemicals and a paid hauler for the rest can cost far less than paying a hauler for everything. Outside the city, Cobb, DeKalb, Gwinnett, Clayton and Henry set their own collection rules, so check before you plan around curb pickup.

Frequently asked questions

Will a garage cleanout company take paint and chemicals?

Some will for an extra fee and some won't take them at all. HomeGuide lists added disposal fees of about $10 to $50 or more for hazardous materials. Ask before booking, and consider dropping them at a household hazardous materials facility yourself.

Is it cheaper to rent a dumpster?

It can be if you have the time and strength to load it yourself, but you're doing all the sorting and lifting. Angi notes that hiring a pro can mean lower rates on dumpster rental and junk disposal along with the labor, so price both options for your volume.

Do I need to be home during the cleanout?

For a haul-only job where items are already sorted and set out, often not. For any service that includes sorting, plan to be there, at least at the start, so the crew knows what stays.
