Gutter Cleaning Cost Per Linear Foot (2026)

Updated 2026-10-13 · Figures are typical estimates reported by the sources cited at the end of this page.

Gutter cleaning is usually priced by the linear foot, and the height of your house matters almost as much as the length. Knowing roughly how many feet of gutter you have lets you check any quote in seconds.

Quick answer

Angi's 2026 data shows a national average of about $168 per cleaning, with most homeowners paying $119–$234. Per linear foot, Angi reports $0.95–$2.25 depending on height. Homewyse estimated about $1.10–$1.35 per linear foot as of January 2026. These are published estimates; your local labor rates will move them.

How to measure your gutters

Walk the perimeter and add up the length of each roof edge that has a gutter. Most houses have gutters on the eaves (the horizontal edges), not the gable ends. Angi notes most homes have roughly 125–200 linear feet. A simple rectangle home 50 ft by 30 ft with gutters on the two long sides has about 100 ft; add porches, garages and dormers.

Then multiply: 150 ft × $1.00–$1.85 for a two-story home gives roughly $150–$280 — a useful sanity check against a quote.

Factors that move the price

  • Height and roof pitch. Angi notes steep roofs can add roughly 15%.
  • Debris level. Gutters that haven't been cleaned in years, with compacted mud or plants growing in them, take longer. Angi reports 10%–50% more for neglected gutters.
  • Season. Fall and spring are peak; Angi says peak pricing can run 10%–50% higher.
  • Downspouts. Clearing clogged downspouts is often extra — Angi lists about $50–$100.
  • Gutter guards. Guards reduce how often you clean, but some types must be removed and reinstalled to clean, which can add time.
  • Disposal. Some companies bag and haul debris; others leave it in your yard waste bin.

How often to clean

Angi recommends at least twice a year; HomeGuide suggests two to three times. Homes under pines or oaks often need more. In the Southeast, a common schedule is late spring (after pollen and seed drop) and late fall or early winter after leaves come down.

Why it matters

Clogged gutters overflow at the corners and dump water next to the foundation. That can lead to fascia rot, basement or crawlspace moisture, eroded landscaping and, in cold climates, ice dams. The cleaning is cheap compared with any of those repairs.

DIY safety

Single-story gutters are a reasonable DIY job with a stable ladder, a stand-off stabilizer, gloves and a scoop. Two- and three-story homes are a different story: ladder falls are a leading cause of home-maintenance injuries. If you aren't comfortable with ladder work at height, this is a job to hire out. Never work near power lines.

What a good service includes

  1. Removing debris by hand or with a vacuum system.
  2. Flushing gutters and downspouts to confirm water flows.
  3. Bagging or hauling debris.
  4. Noting loose hangers, leaking seams or sagging sections.
  5. Before-and-after photos, especially for roofs you can't see.

Gutter cleaning vs. gutter brightening

Cleaning removes debris from inside the trough. "Brightening" or "whitening" removes black streaks on the outside face and is usually a pressure washing add-on. If you want both, make sure the quote lists both.

Ways to save

  • Book a twice-a-year plan; many companies discount recurring service.
  • Schedule before the peak rush.
  • Combine with roof or house washing in one visit.
  • Trim overhanging branches to reduce debris.

Questions to ask

  2. Are downspouts included?
  3. Do you haul debris away?
  4. Are you insured for ladder and roof work?

How to compare gutter cleaning quotes

Gutter cleaning quotes are usually priced by the linear foot or by the story. A fair comparison checks:

  • Downspouts. Make sure each bid includes flushing every downspout, including the underground drain lines if you have them. Clogged downspouts are where most overflow starts.
  • Debris disposal. Ask whether debris is bagged and hauled away or left in your beds. Also ask whether the roof is blown off first so the next rain doesn't wash leaves right back in.
  • Gutter guards. If you have guards, ask whether they remove and reinstall them and whether that costs extra.
  • Height and pitch. Two-story and steep roofs need more ladder setup or roof work. Give everyone the same information so the prices are comparable.
  • Photo proof. Many reputable companies send before-and-after photos, which matters when you can't see the gutters from the ground.
  • Repairs. Loose hangers, leaking seams and sagging runs are usually priced separately. Ask for repair prices to be itemized, not just "fixed while we're up there."

Ask whether the company carries liability insurance. Ladder work around your roof edge is where damage and injuries happen.

Notes for metro Atlanta homeowners

Metro Atlanta's tree canopy is the reason gutter cleaning comes up so often here. Oak tassels and pollen pile up in spring, pine needles fall much of the year, and hardwood leaves drop in late fall. Pine needles in particular slip through many mesh guards and mat into dams. Summer thunderstorms bring short, heavy downpours that overflow clogged gutters fast, and red-clay soil next to a foundation erodes and splashes when water pours over the edge. Many homeowners schedule one cleaning after spring pollen and oak tassels and another after the leaves are down. Frequently asked questions

Do gutter guards eliminate cleaning?

They reduce it, but most guards still need periodic cleaning or rinsing, especially under pine needles and seed pods.

When is the best time to book?

Late spring and late fall are common. Booking just before or after the peak rush can make scheduling easier.

What happens if I skip gutter cleaning for a year?

Debris compacts, plants can sprout, and water overflows at corners and seams. Overflow near the foundation can lead to erosion, wood rot on fascia boards and moisture problems in basements or crawlspaces. The longer gutters go, the more the next cleaning tends to cost.
