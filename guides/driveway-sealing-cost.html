How Much Does Driveway Sealing Cost? 2026 Price Guide

Updated 2026-10-17 · Figures are typical estimates reported by the sources cited at the end of this page.

Driveway sealing, or sealcoating, is a thin protective layer applied over asphalt (and, with different products, concrete). It slows the drying and graying of asphalt caused by sun, keeps water and oil from soaking in, and makes the driveway look new. It isn't a repair. It won't fix potholes, crumbling edges or structural cracks. Knowing what sealing can and can't do is half of judging whether a quote is fair.

Quick answer

HomeGuide reports driveway sealing at about $1–$2 per square foot including labor, or $400–$800 for a typical 400-square-foot driveway, with a national average around $600. It lists material at roughly $0.10–$0.50 per square foot and labor at about $0.90–$1.50 per square foot. Concrete sealing is priced differently, at about $1–$3 per square foot installed according to the same source. These are typical estimates, not quotes.

Measuring your driveway

Because the job is priced by area, measure before you call. Multiply length by average width for each rectangular section and add them up. Include any parking pad or turnaround, and leave out areas you don't want sealed. Long rural or estate driveways are often measured in linear feet times width. If two bids differ a lot, compare the square footage each company wrote down. A surprising number of "price" differences are really measuring differences.

Sealant types

HomeGuide's materials-only figures show sealer is cheap compared to labor, but the product still matters:

  • Asphalt emulsion: common and lower-odor. It resists gasoline and oil stains less well than some alternatives.
  • Coal tar emulsion: long used for its durability and fuel resistance, but it contains polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) and has been restricted or banned in some states and localities. Ask what your contractor uses and whether it's allowed where you live.
  • Acrylic/latex and polymer-modified: generally more expensive and marketed as longer-lasting and more UV-resistant.
  • Fast-dry: lets you use the driveway sooner but, per HomeGuide, needs specific warm, dry conditions to cure.

A related issue is dilution. Sealer can be thinned with water, and an over-thinned coat looks fine on day one and wears off early. Ask what product goes down, at what mix ratio, and how many coats. HomeGuide includes "Do you thin out your sealants?" in its list of questions to ask.

What a proper job includes

  1. Cleaning: edging back grass, blowing or sweeping off debris, and scrubbing or treating oil spots. Sealer won't bond over oil.
  2. Crack filling: hot-pour or cold-pour filler in cracks before sealing. HomeGuide prices small crack and hole repairs at about $100–$400.
  3. Masking: protecting the garage slab, sidewalks, curbs and landscaping edges from overspray.
  4. Application: squeegee or spray, typically one or two coats depending on traffic and condition.
  5. Barricading: blocking the driveway while it cures. HomeGuide cites 12–72 hours before driving on it, depending on product and weather.

When sealing isn't the right call

Sealer is maintenance for driveways that are basically sound. If the asphalt is crumbling, has lots of interconnected "alligator" cracking, or has sunk or heaved, sealing just paints over a failing surface. HomeGuide prices asphalt resurfacing at roughly $1–$3 per square foot and full replacement at about $4–$15 per square foot. A brand-new asphalt driveway also shouldn't be sealed right away. HomeGuide suggests waiting three to six months for it to cure.

How often to reseal

HomeGuide puts resealing at every two to ten years depending on product and climate. That's a wide range, and sealing too often builds up layers that can crack and peel. A practical sign: when the surface has gone from black to gray and the aggregate is starting to show, it's time. If it's still dark and water beads on it, it can wait.

DIY or hire out?

Store-bought sealer in 5-gallon pails makes DIY tempting, and it's reasonable for a small, sound driveway. The hard parts are cleaning thoroughly, filling cracks properly and getting an even coat without lap marks or tracking it into the garage. Professionals use commercial-grade materials and equipment, and their price includes crack filling and cleanup. Get the bids, compare them to your material cost and a weekend of your time, and decide.

How to compare driveway sealing quotes

Sealcoating has a reputation for door-to-door "leftover material" offers, so put bids in writing and compare:

  • Square footage measured. Make sure every bid uses the same measurement.
  • Product and mix ratio. Which sealer, whether it's diluted, and how many coats.
  • Crack filling. Included or per linear foot? Hot or cold pour?
  • Oil-spot treatment and edging. Both affect how well the sealer bonds and how clean the edges look.
  • Cure time and barricades. When you can walk and drive on it, and whether they'll block the drive.
  • Overspray responsibility. Who cleans it up if sealer gets on your garage floor, siding or a neighbor's car?

Be wary of anyone who shows up unannounced, says they have material left over from a nearby job, and wants cash today. Get a business name, phone number and written quote before work starts.

Notes for metro Atlanta homeowners

Asphalt sealer needs a dry surface and the right temperature to cure (HomeGuide notes roughly 50°–95°F for many products). In metro Atlanta, that usually means spring and fall, avoiding the hottest, stormiest summer days. Check the National Weather Service Peachtree City office forecast for a dry window. Red clay washing onto the driveway from beds and lawns stains asphalt and concrete edges. Fixing drainage or adding edging can reduce how often you need cleaning before sealing. Many Atlanta-area driveways are concrete rather than asphalt. Those don't get asphalt sealcoat, so make sure anyone quoting knows which surface you have. Coal-tar rules vary by jurisdiction, so ask your contractor what's allowed locally.

Frequently asked questions

Does sealing fix cracks?

No. Cracks should be filled before sealing. Sealer protects the surface but doesn't repair structural damage.

Can I seal a concrete driveway with asphalt sealer?

No. Concrete uses penetrating or acrylic concrete sealers, which are priced differently.

Are these prices guaranteed?

No. All figures on this page are typical ranges reported by the sources listed below, presented as estimates. Your actual price depends on your property, your location and the provider you choose.
