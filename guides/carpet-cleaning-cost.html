Carpet Cleaning Cost Per Room and Per Square Foot (2026)

Updated 2026-10-13 · Figures are typical estimates reported by the sources cited at the end of this page.

Carpet cleaning ads love a low per-room number. The final bill often looks different once minimums, room-size limits and add-ons are included. Here is what published cost surveys report and how to compare offers fairly.

Quick answer

HomeGuide reports professional carpet cleaning at about $0.25–$0.50 per square foot and roughly $40–$90+ per room for hot-water extraction, with a typical 3-bedroom home (about five carpeted areas) at $200–$500. Angi/HomeAdvisor report a national average visit of about $183, with most homeowners paying around $123–$242, and per-square-foot pricing from about $0.20 up to $0.90 depending on method. These are survey ranges, not quotes.

Hot-water extraction is what most carpet manufacturers recommend for residential carpet, and many warranties require periodic professional extraction cleaning. Check your warranty paperwork before choosing a method.

What counts as a "room"?

This is where most surprise charges come from. Companies define a room by a maximum size, often somewhere around 200–300 square feet. A large living room plus dining area may count as two rooms. Hallways, stairs and closets are often priced separately. Ask for the room size limit in writing.

Common add-ons

  • Pet urine treatment — enzyme treatment or subsurface extraction for odor. Surface cleaning alone often doesn't fix urine smell because it soaks into the pad.
  • Spot and stain removal beyond normal pre-spray.
  • Carpet protector applied after cleaning.
  • Furniture moving — some companies move light furniture free, heavy pieces cost extra.
  • Stairs — usually priced per step or per flight.
  • Upholstery and area rugs — separate pricing; delicate rugs may need off-site cleaning.

Spotting bait-and-switch offers

A classic pattern: an ad for a very low "whole house" price, then on arrival the technician says the advertised service is a light rinse and recommends a much more expensive "deep clean." Protect yourself by asking on the phone: what method is included in the advertised price, is pre-treatment included, what is the room size limit, and what is the total for my exact rooms. Get it by text or email.

DIY rental vs. professional

Rental machines cost less per visit and work for light refreshes, but they have weaker suction and less heat, so carpets stay wet longer and residue can attract dirt. Over-wetting can cause mildew or pad damage. Professionals with truck-mounted units generally extract more water and soil. A reasonable approach many homeowners use: professional cleaning once a year, DIY spot care in between.

How often to clean

Many carpet manufacturers recommend professional cleaning about every 12–18 months, and more often with pets, kids, or allergies. Check your specific manufacturer's care guide, especially if you want to keep the warranty valid.

Preparing for the appointment

  • Vacuum first if the company asks; some include it.
  • Pick up small items, toys and cords from the floor.
  • Point out stains and tell the tech what caused them if you know.
  • Plan for drying time — often several hours for extraction cleaning; run fans or AC to speed it up.

Questions to ask

  1. What method do you use and what equipment (truck-mount or portable)?
  2. How do you define a room?
  3. Is pre-spray and spot treatment included?
  4. What is the minimum charge?
  5. Are your technicians trained or certified (for example, through an industry body)?
  6. Do you guarantee results and re-treat spots that return?

How to compare carpet cleaning quotes

National averages are a starting point, not a price. The most reliable way to know what carpet cleaning costs for your property is to get two or three written quotes and compare them line by line. When you ask for a quote, give every company the same information: the size of the job, photos, access details (stairs, gates, height, parking), and what you want included. Then check each quote for the same things:

  • Scope: exactly which areas, items or tasks are included, and which are excluded.
  • Pricing basis: flat rate, per unit (square foot, room, foot, load), or hourly — and any minimum charge.
  • Extras: surcharges for heavy soil, height, disposal, travel, or after-hours work.
  • Insurance: general liability coverage, and proof you can verify.
  • Guarantee: what happens if you're not satisfied.
  • Payment terms: deposit required (if any), accepted payment methods, and when the balance is due.

The lowest number isn't automatically the best value. A slightly higher quote that includes disposal, insurance and a re-service guarantee can cost less in the end than a low quote with surprise add-ons. Likewise, be cautious of any company that won't put its price in writing, demands full payment in cash up front, or can't give you a business name and phone number.

Frequently asked questions

How long does carpet take to dry?

Hot-water extraction often leaves carpet damp for several hours. Airflow, AC and fans shorten that. Low-moisture methods dry faster.

Will cleaning remove pet urine smell?

Surface cleaning often won't. Urine soaks into the pad, so it may need enzyme or subsurface treatment, and severe cases may need pad replacement.

Are these prices guaranteed?

No. All figures on this page are typical ranges reported by the sources listed below, presented as estimates. Your actual price depends on your property, your location and the provider you choose.
