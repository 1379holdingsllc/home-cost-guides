How Much Does Mattress Removal Cost? 2026 Price Guide

Updated 2026-10-17 · Figures are typical estimates reported by the sources cited at the end of this page.

Getting rid of an old mattress is harder than it should be. It's heavy and floppy, many trash services won't take one at the curb on a normal pickup day, landfills may charge extra for them, and donation centers often refuse used bedding. Your options range from free to a couple hundred dollars, depending on where you live, the mattress's condition, and how much of the carrying you want to do yourself.

Quick answer

Angi reports mattress removal averaging about $100, with most professional removals in the $75–$150 range and overall prices from about $40 to $200 or more. Size matters: Angi lists roughly $40–$55 for a twin, $60–$100 for a queen and $80–$125 for a king. Box spring removal adds about $20–$40 and a bed frame about $40–$100, per the same source. These are typical estimates, not quotes.

Your options, cheapest first

1. Check your local trash service

Many cities and counties take mattresses through scheduled bulky-item pickup, sometimes free and sometimes for a small fee. Some require the mattress to be wrapped in a plastic mattress bag. This is usually the cheapest route if you can get the mattress to the curb. The catch is scheduling. Pickups may be weeks out or limited to certain days, and putting a mattress out unscheduled can get you a code-enforcement notice.

2. Retailer haul-away

If you're buying a new mattress, ask the store or online brand whether they'll take the old one when they deliver. Angi lists this at roughly $40–$100. It's often the least hassle because the crew is already in your bedroom. Read the fine print: some retailers require the old mattress to be bagged, free of bed bugs and the same count as the new one. Some online "bed in a box" brands don't offer haul-away at all.

3. Donation

Some charities and furniture banks accept mattresses that are clean, undamaged and free of stains and pests, but many don't accept used bedding at all. Call before you drive over. A mattress that's sagging, stained or torn won't be accepted, so be honest with yourself about its condition.

4. Recycling

Mattresses are mostly steel, foam, fiber and wood, and much of that can be recycled. A few states (California, Connecticut and Rhode Island) run statewide recycling programs through the Mattress Recycling Council's Bye Bye Mattress program. Elsewhere, availability depends on whether there's a private recycler nearby. Ask any junk removal company you call whether they recycle mattresses or landfill them.

5. Junk removal company

The fastest and easiest option: you point, they carry. Angi's figures put this at about $80–$200 for a single mattress. Junk haulers often have a minimum charge, which is why the per-mattress price can feel high. The best value is to combine the mattress with other items you've been meaning to get rid of so the minimum covers more than one item. Angi notes many homeowners bundle mattress removal with other junk to avoid paying a separate trip fee.

What raises the price

  • Size and weight: king and California king mattresses and dense memory foam need two people and cost more to dump.
  • Stairs and tight turns: third-floor walk-ups and narrow staircases mean more labor.
  • Bed bugs or contamination: Angi notes surcharges of roughly $40–$100 for infested or soiled mattresses, and donation is off the table. If you have bed bugs, tell the hauler. They'll need to wrap the mattress so it doesn't spread pests through your home or their truck. Deal with the infestation itself before bringing in a new mattress.
  • Extras: box springs, frames, headboards and adjustable bases each add cost. Adjustable bases are heavy and may have motors.

Mattress removal for landlords and moves

Abandoned mattresses are one of the most common items left behind at move-out. Property managers and landlords dealing with turnovers often have the hauler take mattresses along with other leftover items in one trip. If you're a tenant moving out, removing your own mattress yourself, or arranging removal before you leave, is usually much cheaper than paying a deduction later.

DIY tips

If you're hauling it yourself, wrap the mattress in a mattress bag before carrying it through the house, both for hygiene and to give you something to grip. Two people and a pickup truck or a van with the seats folded is the realistic minimum. Strap it down flat, since a mattress catches wind like a sail. Before you drive to a transfer station or landfill, call to check whether they accept mattresses and what they charge, and bring proof of residency if the county requires it.

How to compare mattress removal quotes

For a single item, the "quote" is usually a phone price. Make sure it covers:

  • The exact items. Mattress only, or box spring, frame and headboard too? Name each.
  • Minimum charge. Ask whether the price is a single-item rate or the company's minimum load, and what else fits within that minimum.
  • Stairs and location. Mention floors and elevator access so you don't get a surprise surcharge.
  • Disposal method. Recycled, donated or landfilled. It matters if you care where it goes.
  • Bed bug policy. If relevant, ask about wrapping and any surcharge before they arrive.
  • Arrival window. Same-day or next-day service may cost more than a scheduled route stop.

Notes for metro Atlanta homeowners

Bulky-item rules in metro Atlanta vary by city and county. City of Atlanta residents can check bulk pickup through ATL311, while surrounding counties and cities, and private haulers in unincorporated areas, each set their own rules on scheduling, fees and whether mattresses must be bagged. Georgia has no statewide mattress recycling program like the one run in California, Connecticut and Rhode Island, so recycling depends on a local facility or a hauler that uses one. In apartment-heavy areas, ask your property manager first. Many complexes have rules against leaving mattresses at the dumpster and may charge a fee if you do.

Frequently asked questions

Can I put a mattress out with my regular trash?

Often not. Many services require a scheduled bulk pickup or a bagged mattress. Check your local rules first.

Will a charity take my old mattress?

Some will if it's clean and undamaged, but many don't accept used bedding. Call ahead.
