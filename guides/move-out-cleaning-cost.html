Move-Out Cleaning Cost (2026): What Renters and Sellers Pay

Updated 2026-10-13 · Figures are typical estimates reported by the sources cited at the end of this page.

A move-out clean is deeper than a regular weekly clean. The goal is to return the home to the condition a landlord, property manager or buyer expects — inside appliances, cabinets, baseboards and fixtures included. Because the scope is bigger, the price is too.

Quick answer

Angi's 2026 data puts the average move-out clean at about $360, with most jobs between roughly $120 and $420. Larger homes, heavy buildup, and extras like inside-oven or carpet cleaning push totals higher. Many cleaning companies price move-outs either by the hour per cleaner or by a flat rate based on bedrooms, bathrooms and square footage.

Flat rate vs. hourly

Flat pricing is common for standard apartments and houses: a set price for, say, a 2-bed/2-bath. Hourly pricing is common when the condition is unknown. With hourly pricing, ask for an estimated number of cleaner-hours and a cap. A two-person crew working three hours is six cleaner-hours.

What a move-out clean usually includes

Kitchen

  • Inside and outside of cabinets and drawers
  • Inside refrigerator and freezer (unit should be empty and ideally defrosted)
  • Inside oven and stovetop, range hood and filter
  • Inside and outside microwave and dishwasher exterior
  • Countertops, backsplash, sink and faucet

Bathrooms

  • Toilet including base and behind
  • Tub, shower, tile and grout surface cleaning
  • Vanity inside and out, mirror, fixtures
  • Exhaust fan cover

Whole home

  • Baseboards, door frames and doors
  • Light switches and outlet covers
  • Window sills and tracks; interior window glass where reachable
  • Closets and shelves
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped

Not usually included unless listed: carpet shampooing, wall washing or paint touch-up, exterior windows, garage, trash removal, and biohazard or pest cleanup.

What drives the cost

  • Size: bedrooms, bathrooms and square footage.
  • Condition: grease, soap scum, pet hair and smoke residue all add time.
  • Empty or not: cleaners work much faster in an empty unit. Book after furniture is out.
  • Appliances: a heavily used oven can take an hour by itself.
  • Timing: end-of-month dates are busiest; book early.

Protecting your security deposit

Read your lease's move-out section; many specify "broom clean" or "professionally cleaned" and some require carpet cleaning receipts. Take date-stamped photos and video of every room after the clean. Keep the cleaning invoice. If your landlord deducts for cleaning, those records are your evidence. State laws set rules for how and when deposits must be returned; in Georgia, for example, landlords must follow requirements in the Georgia Code (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-30 et seq.), so check your state's statute or a local tenant resource for specifics.

For home sellers

Purchase contracts often require the home to be left "broom clean" or in similar condition at closing. A professional move-out clean is a common courtesy and can head off last-minute walkthrough disputes. Coordinate timing so movers are finished before cleaners arrive.

DIY checklist if you clean yourself

  1. Start top-down: ceiling fans and high shelves first, floors last.
  2. Run oven self-clean (if available) a day ahead and wipe out ash.
  3. Defrost and clean the freezer early; prop the door open.
  4. Use a magic eraser carefully on scuffs — test first, it can dull flat paint.
  5. Don't forget behind the fridge and stove if they pull out.

Questions to ask a cleaning company

  1. Is your price flat or hourly, and what's included?
  2. Do you bring your own supplies and equipment?
  3. Are inside-oven and inside-fridge included?
  4. What is your re-clean policy if the landlord flags something?
  5. Are you insured and are workers background-checked?

Bottom line

Budget a few hundred dollars for a typical apartment or small home, more for larger or neglected properties, and confirm the checklist in writing so it matches what your lease or contract requires.

How to compare move-out cleaning quotes

National averages are a starting point, not a price. The most reliable way to know what move-out cleaning costs for your property is to get two or three written quotes and compare them line by line. When you ask for a quote, give every company the same information: the size of the job, photos, access details (stairs, gates, height, parking), and what you want included. Then check each quote for the same things:

  • Scope: exactly which areas, items or tasks are included, and which are excluded.
  • Pricing basis: flat rate, per unit (square foot, room, foot, load), or hourly — and any minimum charge.
  • Extras: surcharges for heavy soil, height, disposal, travel, or after-hours work.
  • Insurance: general liability coverage, and proof you can verify.
  • Guarantee: what happens if you're not satisfied.
  • Payment terms: deposit required (if any), accepted payment methods, and when the balance is due.

The lowest number isn't automatically the best value. A slightly higher quote that includes disposal, insurance and a re-service guarantee can cost less in the end than a low quote with surprise add-ons. Likewise, be cautious of any company that won't put its price in writing, demands full payment in cash up front, or can't give you a business name and phone number.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a move-out clean take?

It depends on size and condition. Small apartments may take a few cleaner-hours; larger or neglected homes can take a full day for a crew.

Should I clean before or after movers?

After. Cleaners work faster in an empty home and won't have to redo floors after movers track through.

Does a move-out clean include carpet shampooing?

Usually not. Carpet cleaning is typically a separate service or add-on. If your lease requires professional carpet cleaning, book it separately and keep the receipt, and schedule it after the general cleaning so floors aren't walked on while damp.

Are these prices guaranteed?

No. All figures on this page are typical ranges reported by the sources listed below, presented as estimates. Your actual price depends on your property, your location and the provider you choose.
