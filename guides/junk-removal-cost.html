How Much Does Junk Removal Cost? Truckload Prices Explained (2026)

Updated 2026-10-13 · Figures are typical estimates reported by the sources cited at the end of this page.

Junk removal is priced differently from most home services. You are not paying for hours; you are mostly paying for space in a truck plus disposal fees at the other end. Once you understand how a "load" is measured, quotes become easy to compare and easy to sanity-check.

Quick answer

Angi reports a national average of about $241 per junk removal job in 2026, with most people paying between about $133 and $372. HomeGuide lists a typical range of $150–$350 and a national average near $210. A full truck is the big-ticket item: Angi's 2026 table puts a full truckload (roughly 13–17 cubic yards) at $600–$800, while HomeGuide's price list shows full loads around $400–$450. Treat these as published estimates; local landfill fees and labor rates shift them.

How to estimate your load before calling

A cubic yard is 3 ft × 3 ft × 3 ft — about the size of a washing machine. A full-size pickup bed holds roughly 2–3 cubic yards when loaded level. HomeGuide notes that one full junk-removal truck equals about four pickup truckloads. Some quick rules of thumb:

  • A sofa or a mattress-and-box-spring set is often around 1/8 of a truck.
  • A one-car garage cleanout is frequently 1/2 to a full load.
  • A whole-house estate cleanout can be several loads.

Pile everything in one spot, measure length × width × height in feet, and divide by 27 to get cubic yards. That number lets you check whether a quote of "3/4 truck" is reasonable.

What raises the price

Weight, not just volume

Concrete, dirt, roofing shingles and tile are heavy. Many companies cap the weight per load or charge extra because landfills bill by the ton. A half-truck of shingles can cost more than a full truck of cardboard boxes.

Special-handling items

Refrigerators and freezers (refrigerant), tires, mattresses in some areas, TVs and electronics, paint and chemicals often carry surcharges or cannot be taken at all. Hazardous waste such as solvents, pesticides and propane tanks usually must go to a county household hazardous waste drop-off.

Access and labor

Stairs, long carries, basements, attics and items that have to be disassembled add labor. Angi notes labor typically runs about $100–$300 within the total.

Local disposal fees

Landfill and transfer-station "tipping fees" vary a lot by region, and haulers pass them through. That is one reason the same load costs differently in different cities.

Junk removal vs. renting a dumpster

A dumpster makes sense when you are doing a renovation over several days and can load it yourself. Full-service junk removal makes sense when the stuff is already sitting there and you want it gone in one visit without lifting. Dumpsters may also need a permit if placed on the street, and you pay for the rental period whether you fill it or not.

Ways to pay less

  • Donate first. Usable furniture and appliances can sometimes be picked up free by charities or listed for free pickup online.
  • Use city bulk pickup. Many municipalities offer scheduled bulky-item collection; check your city's sanitation page.
  • Retailer haul-away. When buying a new appliance or mattress, the retailer may take the old one for a small fee or free.
  • Consolidate. Bring everything to the garage or driveway so the crew isn't hauling from three floors.
  • Separate scrap metal. Some haulers discount loads with recyclable metal.

Questions to ask a junk removal company

  1. How many cubic yards is your full truck?
  2. Is the quote all-inclusive — labor, disposal and fuel?
  3. What items carry surcharges?
  4. Do you donate or recycle usable items?
  5. Is the final price set on-site after you see the load? (Most are, so get a range by phone and a firm price before loading.)
  6. Are you licensed and insured in my area?

Red flags

Be cautious with very low phone quotes that turn into a much higher price once the truck arrives, cash-only crews with no business name, and anyone who will not say where the junk is going. Illegally dumped items can sometimes be traced back to the original owner.

How to compare junk removal quotes

National averages are a starting point, not a price. The most reliable way to know what junk removal costs for your property is to get two or three written quotes and compare them line by line. When you ask for a quote, give every company the same information: the size of the job, photos, access details (stairs, gates, height, parking), and what you want included. Then check each quote for the same things:

  • Scope: exactly which areas, items or tasks are included, and which are excluded.
  • Pricing basis: flat rate, per unit (square foot, room, foot, load), or hourly — and any minimum charge.
  • Extras: surcharges for heavy soil, height, disposal, travel, or after-hours work.
  • Insurance: general liability coverage, and proof you can verify.
  • Guarantee: what happens if you're not satisfied.
  • Payment terms: deposit required (if any), accepted payment methods, and when the balance is due.

The lowest number isn't automatically the best value. A slightly higher quote that includes disposal, insurance and a re-service guarantee can cost less in the end than a low quote with surprise add-ons. Likewise, be cautious of any company that won't put its price in writing, demands full payment in cash up front, or can't give you a business name and phone number.

Frequently asked questions

Do junk removal companies take everything?

No. Hazardous materials like paint, solvents, chemicals, propane tanks and some electronics are usually excluded or carry fees. Ask before booking.

Is it cheaper to rent a dumpster?

For multi-day renovation projects where you load it yourself, often yes. For junk that's ready to go now, full-service removal is usually simpler and may cost about the same.

Are these prices guaranteed?

No. All figures on this page are typical ranges reported by the sources listed below, presented as estimates. Your actual price depends on your property, your location and the provider you choose.
