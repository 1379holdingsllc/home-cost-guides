Hot Tub and Appliance Removal Cost (2026)

Updated 2026-10-16 · Figures are typical estimates reported by the sources cited at the end of this page.

Bulky items are the most awkward part of any cleanout. A dead hot tub on the deck or an old fridge in the garage won't fit in a trash can and often can't go to the curb. Here's what removal typically costs and the cheaper alternatives worth checking first.

Hot tub removal: quick answer

Angi publishes a hot tub removal cost guide, and 2026 cost summaries generally put professional removal of a portable (above-ground) hot tub somewhere in the range of roughly $150–$800, with in-ground or deck-built tubs costing more because of demolition, electrical disconnect and access work. Check current figures on Angi's page and get local quotes.

What affects hot tub removal cost

  • Portable vs. built-in. A freestanding spa can often be cut apart or tipped onto a dolly. A tub built into a deck or set in the ground requires demolition.
  • Electrical disconnect. Hot tubs are typically hardwired. Disconnecting and safely terminating the circuit is electrical work — in many jurisdictions it should be done by a licensed electrician.
  • Access. A tub in a fenced backyard with a narrow gate or on an elevated deck may need to be cut into pieces on site. Crane removal for very difficult sites costs much more.
  • Disposal. Acrylic shells, wood cabinets and foam insulation are bulky; disposal fees are part of the price.
  • Water. The tub should be drained beforehand. Ask your local utility how to dispose of spa water; some areas restrict draining treated water into storm drains.

Can you sell or give it away?

A working hot tub sometimes sells or can be given away to someone who will move it. A non-working one rarely will, but it's worth a free listing before paying for removal.

Appliance removal: quick answer

Thumbtack's 2026 appliance removal guide reports national averages roughly in the $100–$250 range, with single-item pickups often costing less. Refrigerators and freezers usually cost more than washers, dryers or dishwashers because refrigerant must be recovered before disposal. Prices depend on your area and whether the item needs to come out of a basement or up stairs.

Why refrigerators cost more

Under U.S. EPA rules, refrigerant in appliances like refrigerators, freezers and window AC units must be properly recovered before the appliance is scrapped. Many haulers either have certified technicians or take units to facilities that handle recovery, and they charge for it.

Free and low-cost options to check first

  1. Retailer haul-away. When you buy a new appliance, many retailers will remove the old one for a small fee or free with delivery.
  2. Utility recycling programs. Some electric utilities run appliance recycling programs, sometimes with a rebate. Availability changes, so check your utility's website.
  3. City bulk pickup. Many cities collect bulky items on a schedule, sometimes with special rules for appliances with refrigerant.
  4. Scrap metal recyclers. Washers, dryers and stoves are mostly steel. Some scrap yards accept them; you'd have to transport it yourself.
  5. Donation. Working appliances can sometimes go to reuse stores or charities that accept them.

When paying a pro makes sense

If the item is heavy, in a basement, needs to be disassembled, or you have several bulky things to clear, paying a junk removal crew is often worth it. Removing several items in one trip is also more cost-effective than separate pickups, since most companies price by truck space with a minimum charge.

Safety notes

  • Never try to cut refrigerant lines yourself.
  • Turn off the breaker and have an electrician disconnect hardwired hot tubs.
  • Remove doors from old refrigerators and freezers left outdoors even briefly — trapped children are a real hazard.
  • Use proper dollies and at least two people for heavy items on stairs.

Questions to ask a removal company

  1. Is disposal and refrigerant recovery included?
  2. Do you handle the electrical disconnect, or do I need an electrician first?
  3. Do you cut the tub on-site? Will you clean up debris?
  4. Will you repair any damage to the deck or fence from removal?
  5. Are you insured?

Bottom line

Check the free options first, then get two or three quotes for anything heavy or built-in. Ask every company to state what is included so you're comparing like for like.

How to compare hot tub and appliance removal quotes

Removing big items is mostly about labor, access and disposal fees, so the bids should spell those out:

  • Disconnection. Ask who disconnects the hot tub's electrical supply or the appliance's water or gas line. Hot tub wiring should be shut off at the breaker and handled safely. Some haulers require a licensed electrician to do that first.
  • Cut-up vs. whole removal. Many hot tubs are cut apart on site. Ask how they'll do it and whether they clean up the debris and foam.
  • Refrigerant. Refrigerators, freezers and AC units contain refrigerant that must be recovered before disposal under EPA rules. Ask how the company handles that.
  • Path out of the house. Gates, decks, stairs and fences can add labor. Send photos of the route from where the item sits to the street.
  • Pad or deck repairs. Removal usually leaves a concrete pad or a gap in the deck. Ask whether any patching is included (usually not).
  • Retailer haul-away. If you're buying a replacement appliance, compare the store's haul-away fee against a separate removal quote.

Notes for metro Atlanta homeowners

Many metro Atlanta homes are built on sloped lots, so a backyard hot tub often sits on a deck or a lower patio that's only reachable by stairs or a narrow side yard. That access is usually the biggest driver of labor, so send photos of the route. Disposal rules differ between the City of Atlanta and surrounding counties, and some bulky-item programs won't take appliances with refrigerant, or need it removed and tagged first. Check with your local solid-waste service before putting anything at the curb. For in-ground or built-in spas, ask the contractor whether any permit is needed to fill or cap plumbing and electrical. Rules depend on your jurisdiction.

Frequently asked questions

Can I put an old appliance at the curb?

Only if your city's bulk pickup accepts it. Many require scheduling, and units with refrigerant may have special rules.

How long does hot tub removal take?

A portable tub with good access may take a few hours. Built-in tubs with demolition take longer.
