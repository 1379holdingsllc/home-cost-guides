How Much Does Post-Construction Cleaning Cost? 2026 Price Guide

Updated 2026-10-19 · Figures are typical estimates reported by the sources cited at the end of this page.

When the contractor says a job is done, the house is usually still covered in fine drywall dust, sticker residue, paint specks, sawdust in the cabinets and grit in every window track. A post-construction clean, sometimes called a builder's clean, gets the space ready to live in. It's more work than a normal deep clean because construction dust is extremely fine and settles everywhere, including on top of cabinets, inside vents, and on surfaces you just cleaned an hour ago.

Quick answer

HomeGuide reports post-construction cleaning at about $0.15–$0.60 per square foot for interior cleaning, depending on the level of cleaning, whether it's new construction or a remodel, and location. It lists rough cleaning at about $0.15–$0.40 per square foot and standard or touch-up cleaning at about $0.20–$0.60, with exterior cleanup around $125–$800 or more. Angi reports a 2026 national average around $477, with most homeowners paying $274–$707, and notes debris disposal can add about $100–$300. These are typical estimates, not quotes.

The three phases

On new construction, cleaning is often done in stages as the trades move through. Remodels may only need one or two of them.

Rough clean

Done after drywall, paint and major trades are mostly finished. It covers clearing scrap and debris, sweeping and vacuuming bulk dust, and getting the space clean enough for flooring, trim and fixtures to go in without being ground into grit.

Final (detail) clean

The main event. Every surface top to bottom: high dusting, light fixtures, ceiling fans, vents and return grilles, inside and outside of cabinets and drawers, appliances, removing stickers and labels, scraping paint and adhesive from glass and tile, cleaning window glass and tracks, wiping baseboards and doors, and cleaning and polishing floors.

Touch-up clean

A quick final pass right before the walkthrough or move-in to catch dust that settled after the final clean and marks from punch-list work.

When you get quotes, ask which phases each company priced. A low quote may be for a final clean only, while a higher quote includes all three.

What drives the price

  • Square footage and finish level: more rooms, more glass, more custom cabinetry and more fixtures all add hours.
  • How messy the trades left it: drywall mud on floors, paint overspray on windows, tile haze and grout film take time and specific products. Walkthrough photos help get an accurate bid.
  • Debris removal: cleaners usually remove light debris, but leftover lumber, drywall scraps and packaging may need a hauler or dumpster. Angi suggests budgeting about $100–$300 for disposal if it isn't included.
  • Windows: new-construction windows often have labels, mortar and paint specks. Scraping glass is skilled work, and careless scraping can scratch tempered or coated glass.
  • Floors: new hardwood and LVP should be cleaned with products compatible with the manufacturer's warranty. Ask before anyone uses steam or harsh chemicals.
  • Add-ons: HomeGuide lists HVAC duct cleaning, pressure washing, carpet cleaning and junk removal as common add-ons with their own pricing.

Dust safety in older homes

If your renovation disturbed painted surfaces in a home built before 1978, the dust may contain lead. The EPA Renovation, Repair and Painting (RRP) rule requires certified renovators and lead-safe work practices, including specific cleaning and cleaning-verification steps, for covered work in pre-1978 homes. Ask your contractor whether the RRP rule applied to your job. If it did, cleaning and cleaning verification are part of the contractor's responsibility, not something to hand off to a regular cleaning crew with an ordinary vacuum.

Timing tips

Book the final clean only after the last trade has left, not the day before the painter's touch-ups. Otherwise you'll pay twice. Change or upgrade HVAC filters after the dusty work is done, since construction dust loads them quickly, and run the system on fan to catch airborne dust before the touch-up clean. If you're moving furniture in, schedule the clean before it arrives. Cleaners can work far faster in an empty house.

How to compare post-construction cleaning quotes

The best bids for post-construction cleaning come from a walkthrough, not a phone call. Compare:

  • Which phases. Rough, final, touch-up, or a combination?
  • Detailed task list. Inside cabinets, window scraping and tracks, sticker removal, fixtures, vents, garage, exterior.
  • Debris and haul-away. Is it included, and is there a volume limit?
  • Floor products. Will they use products approved for your new floors?
  • Glass scraping policy. Will they scrape coated or tempered glass, and who is responsible for scratches?
  • Re-clean window. How soon can they come back if trades leave more dust after the final clean?
  • Insurance. Contractor and builder sites often require proof of liability (and sometimes workers' comp) before cleaners can come on site.

Notes for metro Atlanta homeowners

Metro Atlanta's steady stream of new builds, infill houses and renovations keeps construction cleaners busy, so book the final clean as soon as you have a reliable completion date. Red clay is the local headache. It gets tracked in on boots throughout the build and stains grout, carpet and unsealed concrete. Ask for walk-off mats and floor protection during the last weeks of work, and point out clay stains when getting quotes. Humidity slows drying after floor and window cleaning, so running the HVAC (with a fresh filter) helps. For older in-town homes being renovated, review the EPA Renovation, Repair and Painting (RRP) rule with your contractor if painted surfaces were disturbed.

Frequently asked questions

Is post-construction cleaning the same as a deep clean?

No. It includes removing construction dust, residue, labels and debris, which takes more time and different products.

Should the builder pay for cleaning?

Many contracts include a final clean. Check your contract before hiring separately.

Are these prices guaranteed?

No. All figures on this page are typical ranges reported by the sources listed below, presented as estimates. Your actual price depends on your property, your location and the provider you choose.
