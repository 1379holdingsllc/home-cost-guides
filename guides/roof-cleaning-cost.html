Roof Cleaning and Soft Washing Cost (2026)

Updated 2026-10-13 · Figures are typical estimates reported by the sources cited at the end of this page.

Those black streaks on asphalt shingle roofs are usually algae, not dirt, and they're especially common in warm, humid climates. Roof cleaning removes them, and the way it's done matters as much as the price.

Quick answer

Angi's 2026 data puts the average roof cleaning at about $461, with most jobs between roughly $296 and $626. Price depends heavily on roof size, pitch, height, material and how much growth is present. HomeGuide's contractor pricing guide lists roof washing at roughly $0.40–$1.00 per square foot. These are survey estimates, not quotes.

What are the black streaks?

On asphalt shingles, dark streaks are commonly a type of algae that feeds on limestone filler in shingles. Moss and lichen are thicker growths that hold moisture against the roof. Algae is mostly cosmetic; moss and lichen can lift shingles and shorten roof life if left long enough.

Soft washing vs. pressure washing a roof

Many shingle manufacturers and roofing industry groups advise against high-pressure washing of asphalt shingles because it can strip the protective granules and void a warranty. Soft washing applies a low-pressure cleaning solution that kills growth, then rinses gently or lets weather finish the job. Concrete tile and metal roofs have their own care requirements. Before booking, check your shingle manufacturer's care recommendations and ask the contractor exactly what pressure and chemicals they use.

Factors that move the price

  • Roof area. Measured in roof squares or square feet of roof surface, which is larger than your home's floor area once pitch and overhangs are included.
  • Pitch. Steep roofs require harnesses and more careful work.
  • Stories and access. Two-story roofs and roofs over landscaping or pools take more setup.
  • Severity. Heavy moss may need manual removal plus treatment.
  • Material. Asphalt shingle, tile, metal, slate and wood shake each need different methods.
  • Plant protection. Rinsing and covering landscaping adds time but protects plants from runoff.

Add-ons

  • Gutter cleaning while the crew is on the roof.
  • Gutter face brightening.
  • Preventive treatment to slow regrowth.
  • Zinc or copper strips near the ridge, which some homeowners install to discourage algae growth over time.

How long results last

Results vary with shade, climate and roof orientation. North-facing and shaded slopes regrow faster. Many homeowners in humid regions re-clean every few years. Trimming overhanging branches improves sun exposure and slows regrowth.

DIY? Usually not

Roof work is dangerous even on low pitches, and cleaning solutions make shingles slippery. Chemicals can also harm plants and pets if not managed. This is a job where an insured professional with fall protection is worth paying for.

Before you hire

  1. Ask what chemicals and pressure they use on your roof type.
  2. Ask how they protect landscaping and where runoff goes.
  3. Request proof of general liability insurance and workers' comp if they have employees.
  4. Get before-and-after photos, especially for roofs you can't see from the ground.
  5. Ask whether their method is consistent with your shingle manufacturer's guidance.

Is roof cleaning worth it?

For curb appeal — especially before selling — many homeowners find it worth the cost. For moss and lichen, removal can also help protect the roof. If your roof is near the end of its life, get a roofing inspection first; you may be better off putting the money toward replacement, ideally with algae-resistant shingles.

How to compare roof cleaning quotes

National averages are a starting point, not a price. The most reliable way to know what roof cleaning costs for your property is to get two or three written quotes and compare them line by line. When you ask for a quote, give every company the same information: the size of the job, photos, access details (stairs, gates, height, parking), and what you want included. Then check each quote for the same things:

  • Scope: exactly which areas, items or tasks are included, and which are excluded.
  • Pricing basis: flat rate, per unit (square foot, room, foot, load), or hourly — and any minimum charge.
  • Extras: surcharges for heavy soil, height, disposal, travel, or after-hours work.
  • Insurance: general liability coverage, and proof you can verify.
  • Guarantee: what happens if you're not satisfied.
  • Payment terms: deposit required (if any), accepted payment methods, and when the balance is due.

The lowest number isn't automatically the best value. A slightly higher quote that includes disposal, insurance and a re-service guarantee can cost less in the end than a low quote with surprise add-ons. Likewise, be cautious of any company that won't put its price in writing, demands full payment in cash up front, or can't give you a business name and phone number.

Frequently asked questions

Will roof cleaning void my warranty?

High-pressure washing can conflict with some manufacturers' guidance. Check your shingle warranty and ask the contractor about their method.

Do roof cleaning chemicals hurt plants?

They can. Good crews pre-wet, cover or rinse plants and manage runoff.

How do I know if it's algae or a roof problem?

Uniform dark streaks running down the slope are typical of algae. Curling, cracked or missing shingles, bald spots without granules, or leaks inside are signs of roof wear that a roofing contractor should inspect. Cleaning won't fix a failing roof, so if you see those signs, get an inspection before spending money on cleaning.
